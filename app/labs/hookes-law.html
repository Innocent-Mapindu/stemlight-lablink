Hooke's Law: Stretching a Spring

Aim: investigate how the extension of a spring depends on the load, and find the spring constant k from a graph of load against extension.

Apparatus stand · spring · pointer · metre rule · 100 g masses

Load
0 g 0.00 N
Magnified scale
Start with no load. Read where the pointer sits on the rule, type it in, and record it.

Procedure

  1. With no load, read the pointer position on the metre rule with your eye level with the pointer. Record it.
  2. Add a 100 g mass. Wait for the spring to stop bouncing, then read and record the new pointer position.
  3. Repeat, adding 100 g each time, up to at least 600 g.
  4. Calculate each extension: reading − reading with no load.
  5. Plot load (N) against extension (m). The gradient of the straight part is the spring constant k.
  6. Keep loading to find where the graph stops being straight (the limit of proportionality). Then remove the masses and see if the spring returns to its original length.
