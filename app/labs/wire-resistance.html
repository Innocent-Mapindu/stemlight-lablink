Resistance and Length of a Wire

Aim: investigate how the resistance of a wire depends on its length, and use the graph to find the resistance per metre and the resistivity of the metal.

Circuit board tap a terminal, then another, to connect a wire

Terminal A is fixed at the 0 cm end of the wire. Terminal C is on the crocodile clip, which you slide along the wire. Tap the switch to open or close it.

Cells 1.5 V
Build the circuit shown in the diagram below. Close the switch when you are ready.

Circuit diagram and procedure

  1. Connect the battery, switch, protective resistor, ammeter and wire in series. The current enters the wire at A and leaves at the crocodile clip C.
  2. Connect the voltmeter across the length AC: one lead to A, the other to the clip.
  3. Set the clip at 10 cm, close the switch, and record V and I. Open the switch between readings so the wire does not heat up.
  4. Repeat at 20, 30 … 100 cm. Calculate R = V ÷ I for each length.
  5. Plot R against l. The gradient is the resistance per metre. The intercept is the extra resistance of the clip contact and connections.

A-Level extension: resistivity

Resistivity ρ = gradient × A, where A = πd²/4 is the cross-sectional area. Diameter measured with a micrometer screw gauge: 0.32 mm.
