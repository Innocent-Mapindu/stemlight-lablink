Acid–Base Titration

Aim: find the concentration of a sodium carbonate solution by titrating 25.0 cm³ of it with 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid, using methyl orange indicator.

Apparatus burette · pipette · conical flask · white tile

Burette scale
pH probe
–
Read the burette (bottom of the meniscus, to the nearest 0.05 cm³) and type the initial reading. Then hold the tap button and watch the colour.

Procedure

  1. Read the burette at the bottom of the meniscus and write the initial reading to 2 decimal places (ending in 0 or 5).
  2. Run acid into the flask while it swirls. Watch where the drops land: the colour change there lasts longer as you get close to the end point.
  3. Near the end point, add the acid one drop at a time. Stop at the first permanent colour change: yellow to orange for methyl orange, pink to colourless for phenolphthalein.
  4. Read the final reading and record the titration. The first one is a rough titration.
  5. Prepare a new flask and repeat until you have two titres within 0.10 cm³ of each other (concordant). Use their mean in the calculation.
